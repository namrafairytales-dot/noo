# Channel Style Lock

Taken from the channel's best-performing shorts: Million Cab, Hospital Will,
Market Pickpocket and Returned Car (~30k views, good retention). Every new
story follows these rules so the channel keeps one consistent style.

## Voice
- First person, past tense, calm and cold.
- The narrator never describes feelings, only actions and what people said.
- Morally gray, street-smart narrator.

## Format
- One beat per line. Most lines 2–9 words, none over 13.
- No period at the end of a line. Use "…" only right before a reveal, at most 3 times.
- 130–170 words, which is 35–45 seconds of voiceover.
- Simple English a non-native 12-year-old understands. Numbers as digits ("$400K", "200K").
- Use roles instead of names ("my brother", "the manager").

## Shape
1. **Hook (lines 1–2):** a high-stakes event within the first 5 words. Line 2 raises the stakes or jumps ahead
   ("Twenty minutes later, a man with a gun was standing in front of me").
2. **Surface story (~60%):** the narrator looks like the victim or the good guy. Short back-and-forth
   dialogue that plays like a battle of wits. Lists of three for rhythm.
3. **Fake ending:** the surface story seems to close ("I quietly walked away").
4. **Blank line, then the reveal (~35%):** 4–8 lines that flip everything above, often in the
   "X? Y" pattern ("The guy who slapped him? My brother"). Each reveal line points back to a detail planted earlier.
5. **Mic-drop last line:** a rule, tagline or savage sting ("They say thieves don't call… I do").

## Niche
Cons, heists, thieves, money found or lost, greedy family, scams, revenge, power reversals,
set in everyday places (market, cab, shop, bank, wedding, bus, hotel, pawn shop, parking lot).

## Where new stories must beat the references
- Plant 2–3 innocent-looking clues so viewers replay to catch them.
- The twist can't be guessed by line 5 but is obvious looking back.
- No logic holes: every action has a reason the viewer can work out.
- One extra small flip or sting in the last 2 lines.
- No dead lines: every line raises a question, raises the stakes, or pays something off.

## Banned
- **Phrases:** I froze, without a word, my heart sank, blood ran cold, little did I know, the craziest part,
  smiles disappeared, eyes filled with tears, everything changed, to my surprise.
- **Premises:** the 4 reference stories, a rich person or owner in disguise, a fake death or illness to test family,
  a dead person's phone texting back, a lottery ticket, a waiter who is secretly the owner.
