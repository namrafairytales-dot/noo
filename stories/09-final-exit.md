# Final Exit 🚪 (from the movie The Truman Show, 1998)

```
A spotlight fell out of a clear blue sky, right next to me
It had a label on it
The radio said a plane dropped it
Next day, my car radio started describing exactly where I was driving
Every time I tried to leave town, something stopped me
Traffic jam
Then a "nuclear leak"
Back home, my wife started advertising cocoa
To me
In our kitchen
So one night I snuck out and took a little boat
I sailed until my boat crashed into the sky
The sky was a wall
Painted blue
There were stairs… and a door that said EXIT

A voice from the clouds begged me to stay
My whole life was a TV show
29 years, live, since the day I was born
My wife? An actress
My best friend? An actor
Even the sunrise was a switch someone flipped
I bowed and walked out the door
The only real thing in my whole life was me
```

Source: the spotlight, the radio, the traffic jam and "nuclear leak", the cocoa ad, the boat hitting the
painted sky, the EXIT door, the voice from the sky and the early sunrise are all in the film. The last line is mine.

## GenZ funny version (v2)

TITLE: My radio KNEW where I was driving?! 💀

```
A spotlight fell out of a clear blue sky and landed right next to me.
It had a label on it.
The radio said, "Don't worry, a plane just dropped some parts."
Bro, planes don't drop stage lights.
The next day, my car radio started describing my exact route.
"He's heading west."
Who's he? I'm the only one in the car.
I tried to leave town.
Traffic jam.
I took another road.
"Nuclear leak."
This town has more plot armor than a main character.
I ran home and told my wife, "Something is seriously wrong."
She smiled, looked past me, and held up a cup.
"Why don't you try this new cocoa drink? All natural."
Ma'am, I'm having a breakdown and you're doing a sponsorship?
So that night I snuck out, grabbed a little sailboat, and went full speed at the horizon.
Then my boat crashed into the sky.
The sky was a wall.
Painted blue.
There were stairs and a door that said EXIT.
Then a voice came out of the clouds.
"Don't go. You've been on TV since the day you were born. 29 years. Live."
My wife? Actress.
My best friend? Actor.
Even the sunrise had an on switch.
So I bowed, said my usual goodbye, and walked out the door.
Somewhere, the whole world just realized they spent 29 years watching a man eat breakfast.
```

My jokes (not in the film): "Bro, planes don't drop stage lights", "Who's he?", "plot armor", "doing a sponsorship?", the last line.
