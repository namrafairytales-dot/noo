# 02 · Shot breakdown A: "Superpowers Transfer"

2:29 · 107 shots · mean 1.39 s/shot · 243 wpm · camera: 62 % push-in, 30 % dynamic 3D, 7 % locked

**Premise:** when a superhero dies, their power goes to the person they *truly* love most.
**Twist:** the city's unbeatable villain, the Iron Wolf, was the narrator's dad. When the hero kills him, the wolf power passes to the son.

## Script (rebuilt from the burned-in captions; small missing words filled in from context)

> In this world, whenever a superhero dies, their powers transfer to the person they truly love the most. You see, only one
> in a million people are born with a superpower: super speed, fire hands, flying. They get trading cards, parades, their own
> cereal. And every superhero knows that one day their power goes to someone else. Not who they say they love — who they
> truly love. And that caused some problems. When Captain Thunder died, the whole city waited to see his power go to his
> wife. It went to his golden retriever. That dog can fly now. When the Blue Blur died, his super speed went to a lunch lady
> nobody had ever heard of. Turns out they'd been secretly dating for 20 years. And it doesn't just work for heroes. It works
> for villains too. And in our city there's only one villain anyone cares about: the Iron Wolf. A wolf the size of a school bus,
> made of solid black iron. When he howls, every window in the city cracks. He's been tearing up our streets at night for 20
> years. Every hero who's tried to stop him has lost, and nobody knows who he really is. My hero is Captain Valor. I have his
> poster above my bed, his trading card in my wallet. And my dad is the most normal guy in the world. He's a mechanic. He
> takes me fishing on Sundays. He works late shifts, so he's gone most nights. He comes home with his hands all bandaged and
> says "engines bite". One time our truck had four huge scratches down the side. "Raccoon," Dad said. Then one morning Captain
> Valor went on the news. He said tonight he was ending the Iron Wolf for good. Dad turned off the TV. He was quiet for a while.
> Then he hugged me, a long one. "You know I love you more than anything in this world." "Eww, Dad." "Yeah." He left for his
> late shift that night. I sat on the couch with a bowl of popcorn and watched the fight live. The Iron Wolf tore through
> downtown. Captain Valor dove from the sky. They crashed through three buildings. The wolf howled so loud our TV screen
> cracked. Then Valor grabbed him by the tail, spun him around and threw him straight into the river. He sank, and he didn't
> come back up. The news lady was screaming, "The Iron Wolf is dead!" I jumped up on the couch screaming. And that's when my
> hands started to tingle. I looked down. My fingers were turning black. Hard, cold iron spread up my arms, across my chest.
> I grew so fast I cracked the ceiling. My hands turned into paws, and when I tried to yell for help it came out as a howl.
> Every window on our street shattered. I stood there in the wreck of our living room, panting, and tried to understand. When
> someone with a power dies, it goes to the person they love the most. The Iron Wolf loved me. Who in the whole world would…
> I looked at the scratches on the truck in the driveway. I looked at the bandages in the trash can. And I thought about that
> hug. "You know I love you more than anything in this world. Right, buddy?" I called his phone with one giant iron claw. It went
> straight to voicemail. "Hey, you've reached Dad." That night Captain Valor landed in my front yard, ready to fight. But I
> didn't move. I just sat there on the lawn in the middle of the broken glass. He looked at me for a long time. Then he lowered
> his fists, because he'd figured it out too. The worst villain in the history of our city was my dad. And out of every single
> person on earth, he loved me the most.
