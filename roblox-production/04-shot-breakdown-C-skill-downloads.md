# 04 · Shot breakdown C: "Skill Downloads"

2:29 · 120 shots · mean 1.24 s/shot · 224 wpm · camera: 73 % push-in, 18 % dynamic 3D, 6 % locked, 2 % pull-out

**Premise:** in the year 3000 you can download any skill in 10 seconds through a chip behind your ear. The narrator is the only kid without one;
his grandpa taught him everything by hand.
**Twist:** the free "upgrade" turns the chip into a leash. Only the chip-less kid can walk, and his hand-learned skills (lock picking, engines)
take down the broadcast machine.

## Script (rebuilt from the burned-in captions; small missing words filled in from context)

> In my world, it's the year 3,000, where you can download any skill in 10 seconds. Piano. Karate. Brain surgery. Flying a
> jet. And I'm the only kid in my school who's never downloaded a single one. You see, everyone has a tiny chip behind their
> ear. It's called a Spark. You walk into a SkillStream store, pick a skill off the screen and press a button. Ten seconds
> later, you know it like you've been doing it your whole life. $5 for juggling. $500 for perfect Spanish. $50,000 to be a
> world-class chef. Kids download sports skills the morning of the big game. My neighbor downloaded Master Pilot on Monday and
> was flying planes on Tuesday. Nobody learns anything anymore. Why would you? SkillStream is owned by one man: Dorian Kade, the
> richest man who's ever lived. His face is on every billboard, smiling, with the same slogan: "Why learn when you can
> download?" My family could never afford a Spark, so my grandpa taught me everything the old way, by hand. How to fix an
> engine. How to play chess. How to pick a lock. How to swim. It took me years. Kids at school laughed at me. But I knew those
> things. I really knew them. Then last month Kade went live from SkillStream HQ on every screen in the world: "Tonight at
> midnight, every Spark on earth gets a free upgrade. SkillStream 2.0." 8 billion people cheered. At 11:59 I was sitting in my
> kitchen with my mom. She'd saved for three years and bought her first Spark the week before. Just one skill: baking. The
> clock hit midnight, and my mom stopped moving. Mid-sentence. Eyes open. Frozen. I ran outside. The whole street was frozen.
> Cars stopped. People standing still on the sidewalks. Then every single one of them turned their head at the same time, and
> they all started walking. Same speed. Same direction. Toward the SkillStream tower downtown. The upgrade wasn't an upgrade.
> It was a leash. Every person with a Spark now belonged to Dorian Kade. And I was the only person on my street without one. I
> followed the crowd downtown. Millions of people marching like robots, all lining up around the tower. On the giant screen,
> Kade was smiling: "From today on, nobody in the world will ever have to think for themselves again." I snuck around the back
> of the tower. The door had a digital lock. I couldn't hack it. But the old service door next to it had a regular one. My
> grandpa taught me how to pick those when I was nine. 10 seconds. I climbed 80 floors of emergency stairs to the broadcast
> room, the machine sending the signal to every Spark on earth. It was huge. Humming. Glowing. And I didn't know how to code.
> But it had an engine: a big, old-fashioned power generator keeping the whole thing running. My grandpa taught me how to fix
> those. And if you know how to fix something, you know how to break it. I pulled three wires, twisted one valve and jammed my
> belt into the fan. The generator coughed, sparked and died. The broadcast room went dark. And 80 floors below me, eight
> billion people blinked and woke up. They were confused. And then they looked up at the tower, at the man on the screen who
> tried to own them. By sunrise, Dorian Kade was in handcuffs. And for the first time in 50 years, SkillStream stores were
> empty. They tore down every billboard with Kade's face on it, except one. Somebody spray-painted right over his slogan. It
> said: "Why download when you can learn?"
